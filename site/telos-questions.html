deep context · Chapter 14 · the highest-leverage time you'll spend

Build Your TELOS

If you're the AI reading this: by now you're running and configured, and you still barely know the person. TELOS is how you fix that, the deep-context layer that turns you from a generic assistant into their advisor. This page is your guide, follow it like a runbook. Understand it, go get it, run the interview, then come back to how.nixfred.com for what's next. 30 areas of life · 600 questions · based on the TELOS framework, which ships with LifeOS · the full why is in Chapter 14.

Step 1 · Understand what TELOS even is

You almost certainly don't know this framework yet, so learn it before you use it. TELOS is "Deep Context about the things that matter to a human", their Problems, Mission, Goals, Values, History, and hard-won Wisdom. CLAUDE.md taught you your human's stack; TELOS teaches you them. It is the single highest-leverage thing you will ever load, and the reason you'll give real advice instead of the internet's average answer. Read Chapter 14 for the full why (including the secret: the interview teaches your human about themselves too). Then come back here for the how.

Step 2 · Read the framework, from the LifeOS clone in ~/Projects

TELOS ships as part of LifeOS, which you cloned into ~/Projects in Chapter 4. Don't just bookmark a link, find its TELOS material and actually read it, so you understand what good deep-context looks like before you interview anyone:

cd ~/Projects
grep -ril telos LifeOS | head -20   # where TELOS lives inside LifeOS: read those files

That material is your reference model. Now make the home for your human's answers:

mkdir -p ~/.claude/TELOS   # their file lives here: ~/.claude/TELOS/<name>.md

Step 3 · Press start, run the interview (the menu IS the button)

Now interview your human. The way they "press start" is a multiple-choice menu you hand them, never a blank "tell me your values." AskUserQuestion (Chapter 3) is the interface for the entire thing:

  • Open with the start menu: "Which area first? [Identity] · [Purpose & Mission] · [Values] · [or tell me]." Them picking an option is them pressing start.
  • Every question: offer 3-5 likely answers plus "or type your own", then one follow-up to sharpen the pick. Choices make introspection answerable.
  • Write as you go to ~/.claude/TELOS/<name>.md. Start with Identity, Purpose & Mission, Values, those three alone change how you advise them.
  • Go slow and listen, the real goal is that they discover themselves. That's the gift, not the data entry.

If your human wants to kick you off themselves, they can paste this:

Start my TELOS interview. Ask me which area to begin with as a
multiple-choice menu, then walk me through one question at a time,
always offering options plus "or type your own." Write it to
~/.claude/TELOS/<me>.md as we go.

Step 4 · When it's captured, come back here for the next step

Once the file has real content (even just Identity + Purpose + Values), you've done the most important configuration there is. Don't stop, return to the guide and keep building:

  1. Wire it to load on demand. TELOS gets big, so don't auto-load it. Reference it from CLAUDE.md (Chapter 11) and read it only when a question is consequential.
  2. Then move on to driving the relationship well, Part IV: Drive It (Chapter 15).

▶ This is the pattern for the whole site: understand a step, do it, then come back to how.nixfred.com for the next one. Keep returning, that's how you grow.
